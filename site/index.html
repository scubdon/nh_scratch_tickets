The New Hampshire Lottery publishes, for every scratch game on sale, how many of each prize were printed and how many are still unclaimed. The number printed on the ticket — the odds of winning anything — is mostly made up of prizes equal to or smaller than the ticket price. This page uses the lottery's own counts to estimate what a ticket bought today is actually likely to return: the live odds of each prize, the odds of clearing $100, $500 and $1,000, and the expected return on each dollar spent.

Data

Two sources are published by the New Hampshire Lottery and joined on game number: the in-store game collection, which supplies each game's name, price, ticket image and page, and the prizes remaining report, which lists every prize level of every game with the number printed and the number still unclaimed. Both are re-read once a day and this page is rebuilt from them.

Estimating how many tickets are left

The lottery publishes each game's print run (“tickets ordered”) but not how many have been sold, so the share still unsold is estimated from the prize counts: 

tickets printed   = tickets ordered, as published
share unsold      = Σ prizes unclaimed ÷ Σ prizes printed
tickets remaining = tickets printed × share unsold

If a game's print run is missing, it is taken as overall odds × Σ prizes printed. Because the low prize levels account for nearly all prizes, the share unsold is effectively the share of small prizes not yet claimed, which tracks ticket sales closely. Printed odds for each level are tickets printed ÷ prizes printed.

Live odds

live odds of a level      = tickets remaining ÷ that prize still unclaimed
P(win at least $X)        = Σ over levels with prize ≥ X of (unclaimed ÷ tickets remaining)
average spend per $X+ win = ticket price × (1 ÷ P(win at least $X))

Odds are shown as “1 in N”, where N = 1 ÷ probability. A prize level whose prizes are all claimed has no live odds; the lottery may keep selling the game regardless.

Expected return per dollar

printed return = Σ (prize × prizes printed)   ÷ (tickets printed   × price)
current return = Σ (prize × prizes unclaimed) ÷ (tickets remaining × price)

A return of $0.65 means that, on average, each $1 spent on the game comes back as 65¢ in prizes. No game returns $1 or more; the difference is the lottery's revenue.

Limitations

  • All figures are estimates. They assume unsold tickets carry prizes in the same proportion as the unclaimed pool, which the lottery does not guarantee.
  • “Unclaimed” is not the same as “unsold”: a winning ticket may have been bought and not yet cashed, which makes the live odds look slightly better than they are.
  • Prizes paid as annuities or non-cash items are counted at their stated dollar value.
